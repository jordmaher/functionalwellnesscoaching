About Jordan

I was good at the work. I was best at the people.

Jordan Maher

Founder, Seeded in Curiosity

  • Trained in motivational interviewing
  • Years of partnership work in behavioral health
  • Guided colleagues through high-pressure roles
  • Professional Sales and Integrated Marketing Communications, Illinois State University
  • Mental health access advocate

Where this comes from

I've always been fascinated by people: why we do what we do, what holds us back, what finally helps us move. Psychology was my first love. But I let sensible advice steer me toward a more practical path, and I spent years in sales.

Those years taught me a lot, including what I'm actually good at. It was never the selling. It was the people: getting curious about someone, listening closely, and helping them see strengths they couldn't see themselves.

But that was never the main part of the job. Most days were filled with work that pulled me away from it, and I kept arriving at the same quiet question: what would it look like to build my work around what I do best?

Over the past few years, as I stopped trying to fit someone else's mold and leaned into how I'm naturally made, things got clearer. And people started telling me the same thing: friends, colleagues, people I'd mentored. You should be doing this on your own. Helping people get unstuck. Making them feel seen and heard.

I heard it enough times that I finally listened. This is what I'm meant to do with my time.

"Real change starts when someone finally feels seen, not just told what to do."

Why I do this work

I grew up in a town of 700 people where support of almost any kind was hard to find. I learned early what it feels like to figure things out without a roadmap. That's shaped everything about why I care so much about people having someone in their corner.

For years I've built relationships across the behavioral health world and mentored colleagues through demanding, high-pressure work. Over and over I've watched capable people get stuck, not because they lacked ability, but because they were spending their days on work that didn't use it, and no one had helped them slow down and listen to themselves.

How I show up

My approach is grounded in motivational interviewing, a framework built on the belief that you are the expert on your own life. I won't hand you a plan. I'll listen closely, reflect back what I hear, ask honest questions, and help you find what's already true for you.

People tell me I'm warm, and I am. I'm also direct. If I see you getting in your own way, I'll say so, kindly. I believe in faith, in meaning, and in the idea that the hard seasons are often where we grow the most.

A few things about me

  • I believe there's something bigger than all of us that connects us, and that each of us has been given our own strengths and purpose.
  • I take walks outside most days, often more than once. It's where I do my best thinking.
  • In 2027 my fiancé and I are moving into an Airstream to travel the country, and I'll work from the road.
  • I'd always choose one real conversation over a big crowd.

I'm building this for the woman who is capable, curious, and still somehow stuck. If that's you, you're in the right place.
